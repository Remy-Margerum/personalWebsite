NWS Los Angeles/Oxnard coastal waters forecast · four days from the selected water day, extended past the NWS text with an Open-Meteo model outlook (lower confidence)

Pacific bluefin are the cold-tolerant tuna — caught off California from the mid-50s into the low 70s °F. The number that matters isn’t the absolute temperature but the edge: the break where warm bight water bumps against cooler upwelled water.

The italic note under the title is drafted each morning by Claude from the forecasts on this page — a starting point, not a forecast of record. Hover the chart to read the water anywhere — temperature, typical depth, range from the harbor, and whether you’re inside an MPA. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan (one finger pans on a phone once you’re zoomed in), double-click to zoom further, and the dashed rings are 20, 40, and 60 nm from the harbor mouth; runs and bearings (magnetic, var 12.5°E) are computed great-circle from the harbor. Scrub back through the week of NOAA analyses to watch the break move, or forward past the latest analysis: forward days switch to an ocean-model forecast, drawn with dashed isotherms — coarser than the 5 km analysis, so trust the break’s trend more than its exact position. The wind layer follows the day you pick. The dash-dot line is the Channel Islands National Marine Sanctuary boundary and the violet patches are the official CDFW/federal MPA boundaries — solid for no-take reserves, dashed for conservation areas, darkest for special closures (zoom in for names; the troll lines and spot marks are drawn to stay outside the no-take zones, including the Santa Barbara Island reserve that covers Osborn Bank’s crown). Boundaries, depths, and spot marks are still indicative — verify against official charts and current CDFW regulations before you go. A chart for planning, not navigation.

SST analyses: NOAA/NESDIS Geo-Polar Blended 5 km analysis, served by NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP (public open data, courtesy of NOAA) · SST forecast and wind by Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0; sea-surface forecast from the Météo-France global ocean model) · Depths and contours from the GMRT synthesis (Ryan et al. 2009) · MPA boundaries from CDFW ds582 · Shoreline © OpenStreetMap contributors · Spot notes draw on Salt Water Sportsman, BDOutdoors, and Noozhawk’s Captain’s Log.
