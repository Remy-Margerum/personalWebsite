Our Story

An Italian heart, a California home.

I · THE FAMILY

It started with Rosina

Rosina was our great-grandmother — the one who believed no table was full until it was crowded, and no dinner was finished before the second bottle. Her kitchen ran on tomatoes, olive oil, and opinions. When our family finally planted vines of its own, there was never any question whose name would go on the label.

Santi Rosina is a small house, run by family and friends who might as well be. We pick together, bottle together, and argue — lovingly — about blends together.

II · THE LAND

Italian vines, Santa Barbara light

Santa Barbara County is a strange and generous place to grow grapes. The valleys run sideways to the sea, so morning fog drifts in cool and salty, and the afternoon sun burns long and gold. Vermentino, Sangiovese, and Nebbiolo — vines that have known hillsides for a thousand years — take to it like they were homesick for it.

We farm slowly and by hand, on the belief that the vineyard already knows what it wants to say. Our job is not to interrupt.

III · THE TABLE

Wine belongs at dinner

We don't make trophy wines. We make Tuesday-night wines, birthday wines, someone-got-engaged wines — bottles that want food, conversation, and at least one story told too loudly. If a wine doesn't make the table better, it doesn't leave our cellar.

When the tasting room opens, it will look less like a bar and more like the family patio: long table, low sun, something simmering.
